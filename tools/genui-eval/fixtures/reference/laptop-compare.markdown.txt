**For most students, get the MacBook Air.** It's light, completely silent and easily lasts a full day of lectures. Pick the **Framework 13** if you study computer science or engineering, want Linux, or want a laptop you can upgrade and repair for years. The **XPS 13** is the one to get if you need Windows on a tight budget.

One thing to know first: Apple replaced the M4 Air with the M5 in March 2026, and the XPS 13 you can buy today is Dell's cheaper 2026 redesign, so check what's actually on the shelf.

### What I'd buy

- **General studies:** MacBook Air with 16 GB and 512 GB. If you find the M4 discounted, it's a great deal; the M5 is the same laptop with a faster chip and 512 GB as standard.
- **Computer science, engineering, Linux:** Framework 13 with a Ryzen AI 7 350 and 32 GB. Lots of memory helps with virtual machines, Docker and big IDEs, and you can add more later.
- **Windows on a budget:** XPS 13 (2026). Check with your faculty first that its chip handles the software you need, like CAD tools, because it's the slowest of the three.

Before you buy, check whether your course requires Windows-only software (some engineering and CAD programs do), and look at your university's student discounts: Apple, Dell and many campus shops offer them.
