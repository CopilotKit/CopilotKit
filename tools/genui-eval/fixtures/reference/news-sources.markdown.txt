**Short answer: it depends on the setup.** Remote work isn't automatically more or less productive. The best evidence says hybrid work costs nothing in performance and keeps people from leaving. Fully remote results range from clear gains to clear losses, depending on the job.

The pattern: remote work tends to help with **focused, individually measurable work** (calls, patent reviews), especially when people chose it. It looks worse when output depends on coordination, or when long remote hours hide lower output per hour.

### What this means in practice

- Use home days for deep, individual work and office days for onboarding, planning, design reviews and mentoring.
- Junior staff need deliberate support when the team is remote: pairing, code review and regular one-to-ones.
- Measure output, not hours. In the IT study, people worked longer at home, but output per hour fell.

**Bottom line:** a well-run hybrid schedule is the safest choice the research supports. Fully remote can work well, but only for the right kind of work and with deliberate effort on collaboration and training.
