I'd go with a freemium model and three per-user tiers: **Free**, **Team** and **Business**. Each tier has one job:

- **Free** gets people in the door and makes it easy to invite colleagues.
- **Team** is where most of your revenue comes from, so it should be the obvious choice for any team that is serious about it.
- **Business** charges for organisational complexity: many teams, admins, security reviews.

Here's a starting point. Switch billing or change the team size to see what a customer would pay.

At $10 and $20 you sit right in the market: level with Linear and Asana at the entry tier, and well below Asana Advanced at the top. Competing on price alone is hard here, so the free tier and the absence of a seat minimum do more for you than shaving a dollar off.

### Three recommendations

1. **Make Team the obvious pick.** Highlight it, put the features most teams need there, and keep the jump from Free small.
2. **Gate Business on organisational needs, not on core features.** People upgrade for SSO, permissions and portfolio views, and resent paying for basics.
3. **Price AI separately.** If you add AI features, give each tier a monthly allowance and sell more as an add-on, so heavy users don't eat your margins.

For launch, a 14-day Business trial with no credit card and free read-only guests help teams spread the product internally.
