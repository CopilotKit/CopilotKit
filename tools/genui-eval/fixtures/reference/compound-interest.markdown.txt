After 20 years at 7% a year, your €10,000 grows to about **€38,697**: the money you put in plus roughly **€28,697** of growth. That assumes you leave it alone, every gain is reinvested, and nothing comes out for fees or taxes along the way.

### How much the rate changes it

Small differences in the rate turn into big differences over 20 years, because each year's growth earns growth of its own:

Going from 5% to 9% doesn't add 80% to the result, it more than **doubles** it (€26,533 vs €56,044). A handy shortcut is the rule of 72: divide 72 by the rate to get roughly how many years it takes to double your money, about 10 years at 7%.

Two things will pull the real number down:

- **Fees:** a fund that charges 0.2% a year turns 7% into 6.8%, which costs you about €1,400 over 20 years (€37,276 instead of €38,697).
- **Inflation:** at 2% inflation, €38,697 in 20 years buys roughly what €26,000 buys today.

And 7% is an assumption, not a promise: broad stock ETFs have had years of −20% and years of +25% on the way to long-run averages like that.
